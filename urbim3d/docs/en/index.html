UrBIM 3D Guide

UrBIM 3D is an engine for designing and quantifying urban infrastructure networks: sanitary sewer, storm drainage and water networks. It runs inside Civil 3D 2026 and BricsCAD V26, and most of its commands also work in plain AutoCAD 2026.

04Concepts

Why the program behaves the way it does — read once, and everything clicks.

AEvery vertex is a nodeA pipe is a line with two vertices, and every vertex is a manhole or a fitting. So a pipe end can never be left hanging in empty space, and a pipe can never connect directly to another pipe.BThe drawing is the truthA pipe's length and a manhole's position are read from the drawing itself every time, not from a table that may go out of date.CThe drawing opens for everyoneThe program draws with standard AutoCAD objects, so someone who does not have UrBIM 3D opens the file and sees the whole network.
The catalogThe catalog is the list of pipe and manhole types you work with: material, class, diameters and manhole dimensions. It has two copies: a master on your computer for all your projects, and a copy inside each drawing holding only what that drLevels and rolesA level is a surface from which manholes take a value: the finished grade, the natural ground, or any surface you define. A role tells the program what each level is used for — the most important being "depth reference": the level depths arBranch and lineA branch is a chain of connected pipes with no junction between its two ends. It is derived from the drawing and has no name. A line is a named route in a single flow direction, made up of whole branches — and long sections are drawn along The drawing opens for everyoneUrBIM 3D draws the network with ordinary AutoCAD entities: a pipe is a polyline and a manhole is a block. So anyone who opens the file without UrBIM 3D — a colleague, a contractor, a reviewing authority — sees the complete network, with itsOur commands and AutoCAD commandsYou can edit the network in two ways: with UrBIM 3D commands, and the network stays sound at once; or with ordinary AutoCAD commands, and what changed is recorded and applied to the model when you ask for a refresh.Every vertex is a nodeA pipe is a line with only two vertices, and every vertex is a node (a manhole, a fitting, a valve…). Vertices that lie at the same point are one node. This rule alone is what keeps a network in UrBIM 3D from breaking.
